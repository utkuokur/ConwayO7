4.6 The comparison chains

Definition 86 Lexicographic comparison of literal words
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 40
  • Definition 67

Lean declarations

  • ConwayO7.Encoder.LexLeq
  • ConwayO7.Encoder.lexXA
  • ConwayO7.Encoder.lexYA

Fix two sequences \(X, Y\) of \(693\) literals over the orbit variables — for the encoding, \(X\) lists the orbit variables in position order and \(Y\) lists them re-indexed by a generator’s orbit action. A valuation \(a\) satisfies \(X \preceq Y\) if for every \(i {\lt} 693\): whenever the literal values of \(X\) and \(Y\) agree at all positions \(j {\lt} i\) and the \(i\)-th literal of \(X\) is true, the \(i\)-th literal of \(Y\) is true. This is the lexicographic comparison of the two literal-value words under \(a\).

Definition 87 The lex-leader invariant
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 86
  • Definition 67
  • Definition 40

Lean declarations

A valuation of the orbit variables satisfies the lex-leader invariant if for each of the \(101\) generators \(\rho \) it satisfies \(X \preceq Y_\rho \), where \(Y_\rho \) is \(X\) re-indexed by \(\rho \)’s orbit action. On words this says precisely \(\omega \preceq \rho \cdot \omega \) for every generator \(\rho \), i.e. condition (4) of Definition 40.

Lemma 88 Satisfiability of one comparison chain
#

Uses

  • Definition 86
  • Definition 40

Lean declarations

  • ConwayO7.Encoder.specAddLexLeq_sat
  • ConwayO7.Encoder.specAddLexLeq

The clauses rendering one comparison \(X \preceq Y\) use fresh auxiliary registers. Every valuation \(a\) that satisfies all previously emitted clauses and the comparison \(X \preceq Y\) (on literal values) extends, by assigning only the fresh registers, to a valuation satisfying all clauses of the chain as well.

Proof sketch ▶

The chain introduces, at each position where the two literals differ structurally, one register meaning “all earlier positions agree”, defined by clauses from its predecessor and the current pair, together with the implication (\(i\)-th literal of \(X\) true \(\Rightarrow \) \(i\)-th literal of \(Y\) true) guarded by the previous register. Assign each register its intended truth value — the running equality flag of the literal-value words. Every defining clause then holds by construction, and every guarded implication holds because the comparison \(X \preceq Y\) supplies exactly the implication at each position whose strict prefix agrees. Induction over the positions of the chain.

Proposition 89 The comparison section
#

Uses

  • Definition 87
  • Lemma 88

Lean declarations

  • ConwayO7.Encoder.lex_section_trip
  • ConwayO7.Encoder.specAddLexLeq_trip
  • ConwayO7.Encoder.specLexAgainst_trip

The full minimality section of the encoding — the \(101\) chained comparisons of condition (4) — preserves satisfiability: any valuation of the orbit block satisfying the lex-leader invariant extends over the auxiliary registers of the section to satisfy every clause the section emits.

Proof sketch ▶

Each of the \(101\) chains is handled by Lemma 88, whose hypothesis is one conjunct of the lex-leader invariant; the extensions compose because each chain touches only its own fresh registers, leaving the orbit block — and hence the invariant — unchanged.
