Let \(N \le S_{99}\) be generated by: the block transposition exchanging \(7\)-blocks \(0,1\); the transpositions of adjacent blocks \(i,i{+}1\) for \(2 \le i \le 12\); the rotations of each block; and the five multiplier permutations \(j \mapsto aj \bmod 7\) (\(2 \le a \le 6\)) applied simultaneously to all blocks. Each \(\rho \in N\) normalises \(\langle \sigma _0\rangle \), hence acts on the orbit space and thus on words \(\{ 0,1\} ^{693}\) by \((\rho \cdot \omega )(o) = \omega (\rho ^{-1}o)\). The encoding uses \(101\) fixed generators of this action.

Lemma 13 Normalisation of the fixed vertex’s neighbourhood

Every \(\sigma _0\)-invariant \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) is isomorphic, via a block relabelling commuting with \(\sigma _0\), to one in which the neighbourhood of the fixed vertex \(0\) is exactly the union of blocks \(0\) and \(1\).

Proof sketch ▶

The neighbourhood of \(0\) is \(\sigma _0\)-invariant and of size \(14\), hence a union of exactly two \(7\)-blocks; a permutation of block indices carries these two blocks to positions \(0,1\), and any such permutation commutes with \(\sigma _0\) and preserves strong regularity (transport along a graph isomorphism).

Theorem 14 Completeness of minimality
#

Uses

  • Lemma 13
  • Definition 12

Lean declarations

Every \(\sigma _0\)-invariant \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) admits an isomorphic copy whose orbit word \(\omega \) satisfies conditions (3)–(4) of Definition 9: the normalisation of Lemma 13 and \(\omega \preceq \rho \cdot \omega \) (lexicographic order) for each of the \(101\) generators \(\rho \).

Proof sketch ▶

Interpret words in \(\{ 0,1\} ^{693}\) as binary expansions of natural numbers. Among all words reachable from the normalised \(\omega \) by the generator action (a set closed under each generator and preserving both strong regularity and the normalisation), choose one of minimal numerical value; the well-ordering of \(\mathbb {N}\) provides it. For each generator \(\rho \) the word \(\rho \cdot \omega _{\min }\) is again reachable, so \(\mathrm{val}(\omega _{\min }) \le \mathrm{val}(\rho \cdot \omega _{\min })\), and numerical order refines lexicographic order on fixed-length words.
