Let \(\Gamma \) be an \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) and let \(\pi \) be an adjacency-preserving permutation of its vertices with \(\operatorname {ord}\pi = 7\). Then \(\pi \) fixes exactly one vertex.

Proof sketch ▶

Step 0. By the congruence (Lemma 8, \(p = 7\), \(s = V\)), \(|\operatorname {Fix}\pi | \equiv 99 \equiv 1 \pmod7\); in particular a fixed vertex \(x\) exists.

Step 1. Let \(s = N(x) \cap \operatorname {Fix}\pi \) be the set of fixed neighbours of \(x\). By \(\lambda = 1\), the map sending \(u \in N(x)\) to the apex of the unique triangle over the edge \(xu\) is a fixed-point-free involution of \(N(x)\); by equivariance and uniqueness of the apex it preserves \(s\), so (Lemma 8 with \(p = 2\)) \(|s|\) is even. Applying the congruence with \(p = 7\) to the invariant set \(N(x)\) gives \(|s| \equiv 14 \equiv 0 \pmod7\); together with \(|s| \le 14\) this leaves \(|s| \in \{ 0, 14\} \).

Step 2. If \(|s| = 14\) then \(\pi \) fixes \(x\) and all of \(N(x)\), so \(\pi = 1\) by rigidity (Proposition 14), contradicting \(\operatorname {ord}\pi = 7\). Hence \(s = \emptyset \).

Step 3. Suppose \(w \ne x\) were a second fixed vertex. It cannot be adjacent to \(x\) (it would lie in \(s\)). Otherwise \(\pi \) permutes the two common neighbours \(\{ a,b\} = N(x,w)\); neither is fixed (both are neighbours of \(x\)), so \(\pi \) swaps them. Then \(\pi ^2\) fixes \(a\), and \(\pi = (\pi ^2)^4\) fixes \(a \in N(x)\) — contradiction. So \(\operatorname {Fix}\pi = \{ x\} \).

With \(\Gamma \) and \(\pi \) as in Theorem 15, the cycle type of \(\pi \) is \([7^{14}]\): one fixed vertex and fourteen \(7\)-cycles.

Proof ▶

Since \(\operatorname {ord}\pi = 7\) is prime, the cycle type is \([7^{n+1}]\) for some \(n\). By Theorem 15 and Lemma 7 the support has \(99 - 1 = 98\) elements, and the cycle lengths sum to the support size, so \(7(n+1) = 98\) and the cycle type is \([7^{14}]\).
