  • ConwayO7.exists_sigma0_invariant

If some \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) \(\Gamma \), on any vertex set, admits an automorphism of order \(7\), then some \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) on the vertex set \(\{ 0,\dots ,98\} \) is invariant under \(\sigma _0\).

source: ConwayO7/Canonical.lean

Proof sketch ▶

Transport \(\Gamma \) to \(\{ 0,\dots ,98\} \) along any bijection of vertex sets (possible since \(|V| = 99\)); by Lemma 23 the transported graph is again an \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\), and the automorphism pushes through to an order-seven adjacency-preserving permutation \(\pi _1\). By Corollary 16 and Lemma 22, \(\pi _1\) and \(\sigma _0\) have the same cycle type \([7^{14}]\), hence are conjugate in the symmetric group (mathlib’s Equiv.Perm.isConj_iff_cycleType_eq); say \(\tau \pi _1 \tau ^{-1} = \sigma _0\). Relabelling once more by \(\tau \) yields an \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) invariant under \(\sigma _0\) itself.

  • ConwayO7.seven_not_dvd_card_aut_of_no_sigma0_invariant

Suppose no \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) on the vertex set \(\{ 0,\dots ,98\} \) is invariant under \(\sigma _0\). Then \(7 \nmid |\operatorname {Aut}\Gamma |\) for every \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) \(\Gamma \).

source: ConwayO7/Canonical.lean

Proof ▶

If \(7 \mid |\operatorname {Aut}\Gamma |\) then by the Cauchy step (Lemma ??) some automorphism has order \(7\), and Theorem 24 produces a \(\sigma _0\)-invariant \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) on \(\{ 0,\dots ,98\} \) — contradicting the hypothesis. The hypothesis is exactly the statement encoded as a CNF formula in Chapter 3 and refuted by the verified proof checker, completing this branch of Theorem 3.
