5.3 An executable coverage check

The concrete family has \(98{,}536\) members, so verifying prefix-freeness literally — all \({\sim }5 \cdot 10^9\) pairs — is out of reach. Instead the family is sorted lexicographically and only adjacent pairs are compared; the block structure of the lexicographic order makes this sufficient.

Definition 110 Lexicographic order on words
✓
#

  • ConwayO7.lexLe_total

Order \(\{ 0,1\} ^{\le D}\) by \(s \le t\) iff at the first position where they differ (reading left to right) \(s\) carries \(0\) and \(t\) carries \(1\), or \(s\) is a prefix of \(t\). This relation is total, transitive and antisymmetric: a linear order on words.

Lemma 111 Block property of the lexicographic order
#

Uses

  • Definition 110

Lean declarations

  • ConwayO7.prefix_of_lexLe_of_lexLe
  • ConwayO7.lexLe_of_prefix

Extensions of a fixed word \(s\) form an interval: if \(s\) is a prefix of \(t\) and \(s \le u \le t\) in the lexicographic order, then \(s\) is a prefix of \(u\). (In particular \(s \le t\) whenever \(s\) is a prefix of \(t\).)

source: ConwayO7/CoverageCheck.lean

Proof sketch ▶

Simultaneous induction on the three words: matching leading bits are stripped, and a strict inequality at the head contradicts one of the two order hypotheses.

Lemma 113 Soundness of the adjacent-pairs test
#

Uses

  • Definition 112
  • Definition 110
  • Definition 103
  • Lemma 111

Lean declarations

  • ConwayO7.pairwise_of_sorted_adjacent

If a sequence of words is sorted for the lexicographic order and passes the adjacent-pairs test, then the underlying family is prefix-free.

source: ConwayO7/CoverageCheck.lean

Proof sketch ▶

Suppose \(s\) precedes \(u\) in the sorted sequence and \(s\) is a prefix of \(u\), with immediate successor \(t\) of \(s\). Then \(s \le t \le u\), so by the block property (Lemma 111) \(s\) is already a prefix of its neighbour \(t\) — excluded by the test. The reverse prefix relation would force \(s = u\) by antisymmetry.

Definition 114 The coverage check

Given a depth \(D\) and a family \(S\) of words, the coverage check is the finite computation verifying: (i) every member of \(S\) has length \(\le D\); (ii) the family, sorted lexicographically (by merge sort), passes the adjacent-pairs test; (iii) the Kraft weight satisfies \(W_D(S) = 2^D\) exactly, in integer arithmetic.

source: ConwayO7/CoverageCheck.lean

Lemma 115 Soundness of the coverage check

If the coverage check succeeds on \((D, S)\), then every member of \(S\) has length \(\le D\), the family \(S\) is prefix-free, and \(W_D(S) = 2^D\) — the three hypotheses of Lemma 109.

source: ConwayO7/CoverageCheck.lean

Proof sketch ▶

The sorted sequence is a permutation of \(S\) and is pairwise ordered (both facts are standard properties of merge sort, available in the library), so Lemma 113 yields pairwise prefix-freeness, which transfers across the permutation; the length and weight conditions are checked directly.

Theorem 116 Refutation by cells

Let \(F\) be a CNF formula, fix a branching order of length \(D\), and let \(S \subseteq \{ 0,1\} ^{\le D}\) be prefix-free with full Kraft weight \(W_D(S) = 2^D\). If for every \(s \in S\) the formula \(F \wedge (\text{cell } s)\) is unsatisfiable, then \(F\) is unsatisfiable.

source: ConwayO7/CoverageCheck.lean

Proof sketch ▶

By Lemma 109 the cells of \(S\) cover the valuation space; conclude by the composition theorem (Theorem 101).
