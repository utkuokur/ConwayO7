Lemma 72 Adjacency to a block is constant on the block
#

Uses

  • Proposition 37
  • Definition 71
  • Computed Fact 70

Lean declarations

  • ConwayO7.Encoder.adj_zero_iff

In the graph \(G_\omega \) associated to a word \(\omega \), the fixed vertex \(0\) is adjacent to a vertex \(v\) if and only if \(v \neq 0\) and the block bit of \(v\)’s block equals \(1\). In particular the neighbourhood of \(0\) is a union of full blocks.

Proof sketch ▶

Adjacency in \(G_\omega \) is the value of \(\omega \) on the orbit of the pair, and by Fact 70 that orbit depends only on the block of \(v\).

Lemma 74 Exactly two neighbour blocks
#

Uses

  • Definition 1
  • Lemma 72
  • Computed Fact 73

Lean declarations

  • ConwayO7.Encoder.two_neighbor_cycles

If \(G_\omega \) is an \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\), then exactly two of the fourteen block bits of \(\omega \) equal \(1\).

Proof sketch ▶

By regularity the fixed vertex has \(14\) neighbours; by Lemma 72 its neighbourhood is the disjoint union of the blocks whose bit is \(1\), each of size \(7\) (Fact 73). Counting fiberwise, \(14 = 7m\) where \(m\) is the number of blocks with bit \(1\), so \(m = 2\).

Lemma 75 Normalisation of the fixed vertex’s neighbourhood
#

Uses

  • Proposition 37
  • Definition 68
  • Lemma 69
  • Lemma 74
  • Lemma 29

Lean declarations

  • ConwayO7.Encoder.sym1_seed
  • ConwayO7.Encoder.comap_invariant

Every \(\sigma _0\)-invariant \(\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)\) is isomorphic, via a block relabelling commuting with \(\sigma _0\), to one in which the neighbourhood of the fixed vertex \(0\) is exactly the union of blocks \(0\) and \(1\): its orbit word has block bit \(1\) exactly at blocks \(0\) and \(1\).

Proof sketch ▶

By Lemma 74 the neighbourhood of \(0\) is the union of two blocks \(d_1 \neq d_2\). A product of two transpositions in \(S_{14}\) carries \(0,1\) to \(d_1,d_2\); the induced block relabelling (Definition 68) commutes with \(\sigma _0\) (Lemma 69), so the pulled-back graph is again \(\sigma _0\)-invariant and hence an orbit-word graph; it is strongly regular by transport (Lemma 29), and its block bits are those of \(\omega \) permuted so that the two bits equal to \(1\) land at blocks \(0\) and \(1\).
