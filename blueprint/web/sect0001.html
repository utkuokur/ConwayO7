A finite graph \(\Gamma \) on \(n\) vertices is strongly regular with parameters \((n,k,\lambda ,\mu )\) if it is \(k\)-regular, any two adjacent vertices have exactly \(\lambda \) common neighbours, and any two distinct non-adjacent vertices have exactly \(\mu \) common neighbours.

  • ConwayO7.no_aut_seven

Let \(\Gamma \) be a strongly regular graph with parameters \((99,14,1,2)\), on any vertex set. Then \(7 \nmid |\operatorname {Aut}\Gamma |\); in particular \(\Gamma \) has no automorphism of order \(7\).

The proof is a chain of three reductions. First, group theory reduces the statement to the non-existence of a graph invariant under one fixed permutation \(\sigma _0\) of order \(7\) (§2). Second, such invariant graphs are encoded as Boolean valuations satisfying an explicit propositional formula \(\Phi \); the encoding is proved complete: if an invariant graph exists, \(\Phi \) is satisfiable (§3, §4). Third, \(\Phi \) is proved unsatisfiable by a partition of the valuation space into \(98{,}536\) cells, each refuted by a checked certificate (§5, §6).
